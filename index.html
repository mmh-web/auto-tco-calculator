Methodology & Data Sources

Depreciation (typically 40–60% of total ownership cost): Modeled as "% of MSRP retained" at end of each year — the industry-standard format used by Edmunds, KBB, and ALG/JD Power. Year-0 cost basis = your purchase price. Years 1+ projected market value = MSRP × residual %. Buying below MSRP directly reduces your year-1 depreciation cost. Changing MSRP shifts the entire future value curve proportionally.

Depreciation presets — source: iSeeCars 2025–26 study (950,000+ real transactions): Mainstream sedan (Camry, Accord): 84%→55% retained yrs 1–5. Mainstream SUV (RAV4, CR-V): 88%→57%. Pickup truck (F-150, Ram): 87%→55%. Luxury sedan (BMW 5-series): 76%→41%. Luxury SUV (Lexus GX): 81%→48%. EV (Tesla Model 3): 78%→40% — high variance due to Tesla price cuts 2023–24. Sports car (Porsche 911, Corvette): 90%→67%. All values are editable.

Maintenance & repairs — vehicle-type-specific: Each depreciation preset carries its own maintenance and repair cost curves. Calibrated to RepairPal per-model annual averages (2024–25) and AAA 2025 "Your Driving Costs." Examples: Mainstream sedan (Camry/Accord) — $500/yr maintenance, $0 repairs yrs 1–3, ~$350–1,150 yrs 4–10. Luxury sedan (BMW 5/Benz E) — $1,000/yr maintenance yr 1, rising to $4,600/yr by yr 10; repairs $600 at yr 4, up to $3,800 by yr 10. EV (Tesla Model 3) — $250/yr maintenance (no oil changes), repairs ~$300/yr once out of warranty. The base costs shown in the Advanced panel update automatically when you change the vehicle type preset.

Why the TOTAL row = your net out-of-pocket cost if you sell: Depreciation = Purchase Price − Market Value (the value you lose). Total = Depreciation + Interest + Operating = (Price − MV) + I + Ops. Net cash cost if sold = all cash spent − net sale proceeds = (Price − MV) + I + Ops. These are identical — remaining loan balance cancels algebraically. So the TOTAL is your true net cost regardless of when in the loan you sell. The "Net Equity" sub-row shows what you'd actually pocket from selling (market value minus any loan payoff).

Financing: Full amortization — only interest is a cost (principal is equity recovered at sale). The "Remaining Loan Balance" row shows what you'd need to pay off at each year if selling early. Lease cost = all monthly payments + CCR + acquisition + disposition + excess mileage. Leases have no residual equity.

Opportunity cost: Compound annual return on down payment cash invested instead of spent. Default 6% = approximate long-run equity market return above inflation.

Sources: iSeeCars.com (2025–26 depreciation study) · AAA Your Driving Costs 2025 · RepairPal.com aggregate data · Edmunds True Cost to Own methodology · JD Power ALG Residual Value Awards 2025 · BLS Automobile Consumption Study 2024 · CarEdge depreciation data
