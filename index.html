Methodology & Data Sources

Depreciation (typically 40–60% of total ownership cost): Modeled as "% of MSRP retained" at end of each year — the industry-standard format used by Edmunds, KBB, and ALG/JD Power. Year-0 cost basis = your purchase price. Years 1+ projected market value = MSRP × residual %. Buying below MSRP directly reduces your year-1 depreciation cost. Changing MSRP shifts the entire future value curve proportionally.

Depreciation presets — source: iSeeCars 2025–26 study (950,000+ real transactions): Mainstream sedan (Camry, Accord): 84%→55% retained yrs 1–5. Mainstream SUV (RAV4, CR-V): 88%→57%. Pickup truck (F-150, Ram): 87%→55%. Luxury sedan (BMW 5-series): 76%→41%. Luxury SUV (Lexus GX): 81%→48%. EV (Tesla Model 3): 78%→40% — high variance due to Tesla price cuts 2023–24. Sports car (Porsche 911, Corvette): 90%→67%. All values are editable.

Maintenance & repairs: Base curves from AAA 2025 "Your Driving Costs" study and RepairPal aggregate data ($652/yr avg repairs). Combined costs: ~$1,000–$1,700/yr in years 1–5, rising to ~$2,800/yr by year 10. Multiply up for German luxury, down for Toyota/Honda reliability.

Financing: Full amortization — only interest is a cost (principal is equity you recover when you sell). Lease cost = all monthly payments + CCR + acquisition fee + disposition fee + excess mileage. Leases have no residual equity.

Opportunity cost: Compound annual return on down payment cash invested instead of spent. Default 6% = approximate long-run equity market return above inflation.

Sources: iSeeCars.com (2025–26 depreciation study) · AAA Your Driving Costs 2025 · RepairPal.com aggregate data · Edmunds True Cost to Own methodology · JD Power ALG Residual Value Awards 2025 · BLS Automobile Consumption Study 2024 · CarEdge depreciation data
